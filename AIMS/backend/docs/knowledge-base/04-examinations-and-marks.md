# Examinations and marks

## Types of assessment

Six kinds, each recorded as an exam against one subject in one semester:

**Quiz**, **Assignment**, **Mid-Term**, **Final**, **Practical**, **Viva**.

Every exam carries a date, a total marks value, and optionally a classroom and
an invigilator.

## Exam scheduling and date sheets

Exams are scheduled by the administration through **Admin -> Examination**,
which is the same screen that later publishes results. Scheduling an exam sets
its subject, semester, date and total marks, and may also reserve a classroom
and name an invigilator.

**AIMS does not publish a separate date sheet screen.** There is no date sheet
document in the system and no Exams page in the student portal. An exam becomes
visible to a student through the subject it belongs to, and the consolidated
schedule for a term is issued by the examinations section outside AIMS. A
student who wants the full schedule should ask the examinations section; the
date held in AIMS against a single exam is not a substitute for it.

Teachers see the exams for the subjects they teach; administrators see every
exam. Neither can change an exam a student has already been marked against
without the examinations section, because the mark is attached to the exam.

## The three states of a mark

This is the single most important thing to understand about marks in AIMS.

| Status | Who can see it | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Draft** | The entering teacher only | Being worked on, not final |
| **Verified** | Teachers and admins | Checked, not yet released |
| **Published** | Everyone, including the student | Released as a result |

**Students see published marks only.** A mark that has been entered and even
verified is not visible to the student until it is published.

This is why "I sat the exam but see no marks" is usually not an error. There
are three genuinely different situations, and they should not be confused:

1. The exam has not been marked yet — no record exists.
2. Marks are entered but not published — the result exists but is not released.
3. Marks are published — the student can see them.

The assistant distinguishes these rather than reporting "no marks found" for
all three.

## Entering marks

Teachers enter marks through **Faculty → Marks**, for the classes they teach.
Marks are entered as a number out of the exam's total. A mark cannot be
negative, and the exam total must be greater than zero.

Verification and publication are separate steps, so a second pair of eyes can
check before students see anything.

## Percentages and grades

A mark's percentage is `obtained ÷ total × 100`, computed per exam. The grade
letter is then resolved from the institute's grading table.

Where a subject has several assessments, the subject percentage is the **average
of each exam's own percentage** — so a 10-mark quiz does not carry the same
weight as a 100-mark final.

## Class performance

Teachers and admins can see, per class: how many students were assessed, the
average percentage, and the pass rate. These figures are computed from
**published marks only** — unreleased marks never appear in a class average.

## Common questions

**Can a teacher change a mark after publishing?** Yes, but it is an
administrative correction and should be raised with the subject teacher and the
examination section.

**Who decides the pass mark?** It comes from the institute's grading table,
not from a fixed number in the software. See *Results, GPA and Grading*.
